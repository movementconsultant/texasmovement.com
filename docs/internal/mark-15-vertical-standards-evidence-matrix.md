# Mark 15 (internal) — Vertical Standards Evidence Matrix

Internal-only. Not rendered, not imported, not linked from any public route.
Full 12-area detail for `docs/mark-15-external-standards-adoption-framework.md`
Part B. Every field below is populated with *categories and boundaries*, not
actual adopted standards — no external source has been researched, selected,
or adopted in this pass (see the parent doc's non-negotiable restrictions).
Every area's current status: **no standard adopted, no candidate reviewed,
public wording prohibited by default.**

Field key used per area: Intended internal standard categories · Example
evidence types required · Risk level · Public-claim prohibition level ·
Required owner decision · Required operational prerequisites · Required
professional review · Release blockers · Revalidation cadence · Public-surface
classification.

## 1. Consulting

- **Intended internal standard categories:** digital-consulting ethics and
  engagement-scope control; accessibility (WCAG-aligned internal target);
  privacy/data minimization for client materials; security baseline for
  client data handling; documentation and change-control discipline;
  project-acceptance criteria, responsibility boundaries, and client
  communication cadence.
- **Example evidence types required:** a completed engagement record with
  client-approved scope; an internal accessibility self-check against a
  named WCAG success-criteria set; a data-handling policy document; a
  signed engagement agreement template.
- **Risk level:** Medium — reputational and contractual risk if scope or
  outcome claims outrun reality; no medical/legal/life-safety exposure.
- **Public-claim prohibition level:** Prohibited by default. No "we follow
  [standard]," no guaranteed outcome, no implied certification.
- **Required owner decision:** Whether to adopt any named methodology
  internally, and separately, whether/when any resulting capability
  statement may become public (two distinct decisions, never combined).
- **Required operational prerequisites:** An actual engagement-intake
  process, a scope-change process, and a client-communication policy must
  exist and be followed before any public capability claim is considered.
- **Required professional review:** None mandatory at the "internal
  best-practice baseline" tier; a legal review is required before any
  contract-template language or guaranteed-outcome-adjacent wording is
  finalized.
- **Release blockers:** No case-study candidate has cleared the proof
  registry (`docs/mark-5-tmi-content-model-and-proof-registry.md`); no
  engagement-volume or client-count claim has any evidence artifact.
- **Revalidation cadence:** Annual, or immediately upon any change to the
  underlying methodology or client-facing scope.
- **Public-surface classification:** TMI route (`/consulting`, currently
  posture "Building — not yet live").

## 2. Performance (general non-clinical education boundary)

- **Intended internal standard categories:** general non-clinical strength,
  conditioning, movement, and sprint-education boundaries; training-safety
  and progression discipline; coaching/participant screening; emergency
  escalation procedure; referral boundaries to licensed professionals.
- **Example evidence types required:** a written scope-of-practice
  statement distinguishing education from coaching from clinical care; an
  emergency-action-plan document; a participant-screening intake form
  design (never a live form — design only).
- **Risk level:** High — physical-safety exposure if scope boundaries are
  unclear, even for purely educational content.
- **Public-claim prohibition level:** Prohibited by default. No coaching
  availability, no training-outcome claim, no implied medical/therapeutic
  benefit.
- **Required owner decision:** Whether Performance publishes *educational
  content only* or ever offers a coached/supervised service — these are
  different risk classes and require separate, explicit owner sign-off.
- **Required operational prerequisites:** A documented scope-of-practice
  statement, an emergency-escalation procedure, and (if any supervised
  activity is ever offered) insurance and facility-safety review — none of
  which currently exist.
- **Required professional review:** A credentialed strength/conditioning or
  sports-medicine professional must review any content or program design
  before public release; legal review of any liability/waiver language
  before any supervised activity is offered.
- **Release blockers:** No coach credential has been verified; no
  scope-of-practice document exists; `docs/mark-2-12-item-ecosystem-audit.md`'s
  predecessor findings already flagged legacy performance/medical-adjacent
  copy for legal review before any future expansion (see
  `docs/ecosystem-release-matrix.md`).
- **Revalidation cadence:** Annual, or immediately upon any change to
  activity type or supervision model.
- **Public-surface classification:** TMI route (`/performance`, currently
  posture "Building — not yet live").

## 3. Tensegrity / sprint technique / athlete therapy — restricted domain

This area gets a stricter treatment than any other in this matrix, per the
brief's explicit instruction.

- **Intended internal standard categories:** none adopted. Tensegrity is
  treated **strictly as a conceptual/modeling lens** (a way of thinking
  about structural load distribution) — not a clinical framework, not a
  treatment method, not a TMI proprietary technique — unless and until the
  owner supplies qualified evidence and separately approves a specific use.
  Sprint-technique *education* (distinct from therapy) may draw on Tier 3
  best-practice baselines under the same boundary rules as Performance,
  above. Therapy, treatment, recovery, injury management, diagnosis,
  rehabilitation, clinical care, and therapeutic intervention are
  classified **restricted/high-risk domains** in their entirety — no
  internal best-practice baseline is proposed for them by this framework.
- **Example evidence types required (if ever pursued):** a licensed
  clinician's qualified evidence submission; documented scope-of-practice
  and licensure; malpractice/liability insurance confirmation; a
  jurisdiction-specific legal review; informed-consent documentation
  design.
- **Risk level:** **Highest in this matrix.** Direct physical/medical-harm
  exposure, licensure exposure, and legal exposure if any boundary is
  crossed even inadvertently.
- **Public-claim prohibition level:** **Absolute prohibition**, pending a
  full specialist and legal review — stricter than "prohibited by default"
  used elsewhere. No public content, service, app, program, or claim
  touching tensegrity-as-therapy, athlete therapy, or clinical
  intervention may be considered until credential, licensure, insurance,
  scope-of-practice, referral, emergency, consent, and jurisdiction review
  are all separately complete.
- **Required owner decision:** Whether TMI ever pursues a therapy/clinical
  offering at all — a foundational business decision, not a content
  decision, and one this framework does not make or recommend either way.
- **Required operational prerequisites:** Licensed practitioner(s),
  malpractice insurance, a documented scope-of-practice, an
  informed-consent process, an emergency-referral pathway, and
  jurisdiction-specific legal clearance — none of which exist today.
- **Required professional review:** Licensed clinical professional review
  **and** legal review, both mandatory, before any single public sentence
  in this domain.
- **Release blockers:** Everything in "required operational prerequisites"
  above, in full, plus explicit owner authorization to pursue the domain
  at all. **This framework does not and must not establish a TMI
  proprietary technique or therapy framework** — that instruction is
  treated as absolute, not aspirational.
- **Revalidation cadence:** Not applicable until the domain is formally
  opened by the owner; if ever opened, revalidation would be at minimum
  annual and upon any change in licensure, jurisdiction, or scope.
- **Public-surface classification:** No public surface. Private
  research/consideration only, if pursued at all.

## 4. Health

- **Intended internal standard categories:** privacy of any health-adjacent
  research material; scope-of-practice boundary (editorial/research vs.
  clinical); evidence-review discipline for any cited research; informed
  consent (if any future data collection is considered); medical-boundary
  statement; corrections process for published research summaries;
  referral boundaries to licensed care.
- **Example evidence types required (if ever pursued):** a documented
  editorial-review process for health-research summaries; a named
  reviewing clinician or credentialed research reviewer; a
  privacy-by-design assessment if any user data is ever collected.
- **Risk level:** **Highest in this matrix**, alongside item 3 — medical
  misinformation risk, privacy risk if any health data is ever involved,
  and regulatory exposure.
- **Public-claim prohibition level:** **Absolute prohibition** on
  diagnosis, treatment, clinical-service, supplement, recovery, or
  therapeutic representation of any kind, pending specialist and legal
  review, per the parent brief's explicit restriction.
- **Required owner decision:** Whether Health ever becomes a public
  editorial/research property, a private research function only, or stays
  dormant — three materially different risk postures.
- **Required operational prerequisites:** A named editorial-review
  process, a credentialed reviewer, and a corrections/retraction policy,
  none of which exist today; this route is currently "Building/Private"
  per `docs/ecosystem-release-matrix.md`, already flagged there for "a real
  legal/medical-compliance review... heavier than the generic stub
  review."
- **Required professional review:** Legal and, for any research-summary
  content, a credentialed subject-matter reviewer — both mandatory before
  any public release consideration.
- **Release blockers:** No editorial-review process exists; no reviewer is
  named; no legal review has occurred; the vertical remains Private with
  no public route content beyond the existing generic stub.
- **Revalidation cadence:** Not applicable until the domain is formally
  opened; if opened, at minimum annual thereafter.
- **Public-surface classification:** No public surface currently (private
  research only).

## 5. Media

- **Intended internal standard categories:** editorial standards; source
  verification; attribution and copyright discipline; corrections/takedown
  process; accessibility (captions, transcripts, alt text); privacy and
  tracking discipline for any embedded/linked platform content;
  sensitive-topic review process; platform-link and embed governance
  (already partially implemented via the existing Mark 9–14 media-source
  governance system — see `docs/mark-11-tmm-preview-card-ux-optimization.md`
  and the Latest Signal "Ticker Tape Guardrails").
- **Example evidence types required:** a documented source-verification
  checklist (a version already exists:
  `docs/mark-8-tmm-source-verification-checklist.md`); an
  accessibility-compliance self-check for published media; a corrections
  log.
- **Risk level:** Medium — reputational/accuracy risk, not physical-safety
  risk.
- **Public-claim prohibition level:** Prohibited by default. No claim of
  publication cadence, reach, independence, or journalistic authority
  without an actual evidence artifact.
- **Required owner decision:** Whether to formally adopt an editorial code
  of conduct internally, and separately, whether any editorial-standards
  statement becomes public copy.
- **Required operational prerequisites:** A documented corrections process
  and a named editorial reviewer, beyond what currently exists (the
  existing source-destination governance covers *which links appear*, not
  yet a full editorial-standards program).
- **Required professional review:** None mandatory at the internal
  best-practice tier; legal review recommended before any
  copyright/attribution-policy language is finalized publicly.
- **Release blockers:** No corrections process is documented; no editorial
  code has been adopted internally, let alone reviewed for public release.
- **Revalidation cadence:** Annual.
- **Public-surface classification:** TMI route (`/media`, currently posture
  "Building — not yet live," with the confirmed source-destination index
  and Latest Signal rail already governed separately from this framework).

## 9. HERO / commerce (external storefront)

- **Intended internal standard categories:** product-information accuracy;
  consumer-facing policy clarity; accessibility; checkout usability;
  payment and privacy boundaries; inventory/fulfillment/support
  representation; returns/refunds policy; product safety and
  performance-claim controls.
- **Example evidence types required:** an authenticated owner audit of the
  actual external storefront against each category above — this cannot be
  satisfied by an internal best-practice baseline alone, because HERO is
  externally operated and internally unaudited.
- **Risk level:** High — consumer-protection, payment, and product-safety
  exposure, compounded by the fact this hub does not control or audit the
  actual storefront.
- **Public-claim prohibition level:** Prohibited by default, and stricter
  than most other areas: **no assertion that the external storefront
  presently meets any particular practice may appear without an
  authenticated owner audit** — mirrors the existing, deliberately
  strictest proof-registry row (`PR-HERO-001` in
  `docs/mark-5-tmi-content-model-and-proof-registry.md`), which already
  has empty `allowedRoutes`/`allowedFormats` arrays.
- **Required owner decision:** Whether/when to commission an authenticated
  audit of the HERO storefront against each category above.
- **Required operational prerequisites:** The audit itself — nothing else
  in this domain can proceed before it.
- **Required professional review:** A qualified e-commerce/consumer-
  protection auditor for the storefront audit; legal review of any
  resulting public bridge content.
- **Release blockers:** No audit has occurred; `PR-HERO-001`'s prohibition
  clause remains fully in force.
- **Revalidation cadence:** Annual after any audit, or immediately upon any
  storefront change.
- **Public-surface classification:** External storefront / internally
  unaudited (existing `/hero` route posture, unchanged by this pass).

# Mark 15 (internal) — Public Claims Boundary Guide

Internal-only. Not rendered, not imported, not linked from any public route.
A practical reference for anyone drafting or reviewing copy across TMI's
verticals — separates five categories of language and gives abstract
boundary-pattern examples per operating area. **These are illustrative
patterns, not draft public copy** — none of the example phrases below are
approved, proposed, or intended for direct use anywhere on the site.

## The five categories

1. **Safe internal planning language.** Describes what the team is
   *considering, designing, or building internally*. Never implies present
   public availability. Safe to write in internal documents, planning
   notes, and this framework itself.
2. **Potential future public language requiring approval.** Could become
   public *after* real evidence exists and the owner explicitly approves
   the specific wording (per Tier 4 of the adoption hierarchy in
   `docs/mark-15-external-standards-adoption-framework.md`). Never publish
   without that approval, no matter how reasonable it sounds.
3. **Prohibited public language without direct evidence.** Must not appear
   in any public route, ever, until a specific Tier 1 or approved Tier 2
   evidence artifact exists for that exact claim.
4. **High-risk language requiring specialist/legal/clinical review.**
   A stricter subset of (3) — requires licensed-professional and/or legal
   sign-off *in addition to* ordinary evidence, because the subject matter
   carries physical-safety, medical, legal, or regulatory risk.
5. **Statements that must never be inferred from "industry best
   practices."** A best-practice baseline (Tier 3) is an internal operating
   discipline. It is never, by itself, sufficient grounds for any public
   claim of compliance, certification, effectiveness, safety guarantee, or
   professional endorsement — that inference is the single most common
   failure mode this guide exists to prevent.

## Per-area examples

Each area lists one example per applicable category. Bracketed placeholders
like `[X]` mark where a real fact would need to go — none are filled in,
because filling them in is exactly the boundary this guide protects.

### Consulting

1. *Safe internal:* "We are evaluating a documented engagement-intake
   process based on general industry practice."
2. *Future, requires approval:* "[N] client engagements completed" — only
   after a specific, evidenced count exists and the owner approves the
   exact wording.
3. *Prohibited without evidence:* Any specific client count, industry
   name, or engagement outcome not yet in the proof registry.
4. *High-risk, needs review:* Any wording implying a guaranteed business
   outcome or ROI figure — requires legal review regardless of evidence
   status.
5. *Never inferred from best practice alone:* "Certified" or
   "industry-standard" methodology language — a best-practice baseline is
   not a certification.

### Performance / sprint / tensegrity

1. *Safe internal:* "Internal reference materials on general conditioning
   principles are being reviewed for accuracy and scope."
2. *Future, requires approval:* General educational content describing
   *concepts*, once reviewed by a credentialed professional and approved —
   still never a claim of coaching availability or outcome.
3. *Prohibited without evidence:* Any claim of training results, athlete
   outcomes, or program effectiveness.
4. *High-risk, needs review:* **All tensegrity-as-therapy, athlete-therapy,
   injury, recovery, or rehabilitation content** — absolute prohibition
   pending licensed-clinical and legal review per matrix item 3; no
   exception.
5. *Never inferred from best practice alone:* Any implication that reading
   general conditioning best practices qualifies as receiving individual
   coaching, a program, or medical guidance.

### Health / therapy

1. *Safe internal:* "We are documenting an editorial-review process for
   any future health-research summaries."
2. *Future, requires approval:* Not currently applicable — see
   matrix item 4; this domain has no approved path to public language at
   this time.
3. *Prohibited without evidence:* Any health claim, statistic, or research
   summary presented as settled fact.
4. *High-risk, needs review:* **All diagnosis, treatment, clinical-service,
   supplement, recovery, or therapeutic representation** — absolute
   prohibition pending specialist and legal review; no exception.
5. *Never inferred from best practice alone:* Any implication that citing
   general health best practices constitutes medical advice, a clinical
   service, or a safety guarantee.

### Media

1. *Safe internal:* "We are reviewing source-verification and corrections
   process design against general editorial standards."
2. *Future, requires approval:* A specific publication-cadence or reach
   statement, once an actual analytics/records artifact exists and is
   owner-approved.
3. *Prohibited without evidence:* Any specific view count, subscriber
   count, or reach claim not already governed by the existing media-source
   confirmation system.
4. *High-risk, needs review:* Coverage of any sensitive or legally
   fraught topic — requires editorial and, where relevant, legal review
   before publication regardless of source quality.
5. *Never inferred from best practice alone:* "Independent journalism" or
   "verified reporting" language — a documented editorial process is not,
   by itself, evidence of independence or verification.

### Distribution

1. *Safe internal:* "A rights-management checklist based on general
   licensing best practice is under internal review."
2. *Future, requires approval:* A specific platform-reach or catalog-size
   statement, once evidenced and approved.
3. *Prohibited without evidence:* Any audience, reach, or growth figure.
4. *High-risk, needs review:* Any rights/licensing representation made to
   a third party — requires legal review before use.
5. *Never inferred from best practice alone:* Any implied algorithm
   advantage, guaranteed distribution, or revenue outcome.

### Reparations

1. *Safe internal:* "A citation and archival-source standard based on
   general historical-research practice is being documented."
2. *Future, requires approval:* A specific historical claim, once sourced,
   cited, legally reviewed, and owner-approved for the exact wording.
3. *Prohibited without evidence:* Any historical or factual claim without
   a cited source.
4. *High-risk, needs review:* Any claim with legal, political, or
   restitution implications — mandatory legal review regardless of source
   quality; never presented as settled fact without qualification.
5. *Never inferred from best practice alone:* Treating a general citation
   standard as proof that a specific contested historical claim is
   accurate or legally established.

### Social / Gather

1. *Safe internal:* "Event-safety and moderation-policy templates based on
   general community-safety practice are being drafted."
2. *Future, requires approval:* A specific event date, venue, or
   registration path, once real operations, insurance, and safety planning
   exist.
3. *Prohibited without evidence:* Any event, membership, or attendee
   promise.
4. *High-risk, needs review:* Any in-person gathering or ticketed event —
   requires legal, insurance, and safety-planning review before any public
   announcement.
5. *Never inferred from best practice alone:* Assuming a moderation-policy
   draft means a community currently exists or is currently moderated.

### HERO / commerce

1. *Safe internal:* "A storefront-audit checklist based on general
   e-commerce best practice has been drafted for future use."
2. *Future, requires approval:* Any statement about HERO's actual current
   practices — only after the authenticated owner audit required by matrix
   item 9 is complete.
3. *Prohibited without evidence:* Any product, pricing, inventory,
   shipping, return, or support claim about HERO.
4. *High-risk, needs review:* Any product-safety or performance claim —
   requires legal and product-safety review, plus the storefront audit,
   before consideration.
5. *Never inferred from best practice alone:* Assuming that a general
   e-commerce best-practice checklist existing internally means HERO's
   actual storefront currently follows it — it has not been audited.

### Speaking engagements

1. *Safe internal:* "A booking-agreement template based on general
   industry practice is being drafted."
2. *Future, requires approval:* A specific availability window or fee
   range, once a real booking process and agreement exist.
3. *Prohibited without evidence:* Any availability, rate, or booking
   claim.
4. *High-risk, needs review:* Any credential or result claim used to
   solicit bookings — requires verification and, if audience-safety-
   relevant, legal review.
5. *Never inferred from best practice alone:* Assuming a booking-template
   draft means speaking engagements are currently bookable.

### Apps / services

1. *Safe internal:* "Privacy-by-design and data-minimization principles
   based on general best practice are informing early internal planning."
2. *Future, requires approval:* Not currently applicable — no product
   exists (matrix item 11).
3. *Prohibited without evidence:* Any claim that an app or online service
   currently exists, is available, or has any user base.
4. *High-risk, needs review:* Any claim about data handling, security, or
   privacy protections — requires security and privacy/legal review before
   any product is built, let alone described publicly.
5. *Never inferred from best practice alone:* Assuming that internally
   documenting privacy-by-design principles means a compliant product
   currently exists.

### Checkout / conversion

1. *Safe internal:* "General payment-transparency and refund-policy best
   practices are documented for potential future use."
2. *Future, requires approval:* Not currently applicable — no checkout
   exists anywhere in this ecosystem (matrix item 12), and none is
   activated by this or any pass to date.
3. *Prohibited without evidence:* Any pricing, fee, refund, or payment
   claim on any TMI hub route.
4. *High-risk, needs review:* Any future checkout implementation —
   requires legal, payments-compliance, and security review before
   activation, not after.
5. *Never inferred from best practice alone:* Assuming that documenting a
   refund-policy best practice means a checkout, order, or payment flow
   currently exists — none does.

## How to use this guide

When drafting or reviewing any public-facing sentence anywhere in this
ecosystem: identify which of the five categories it falls into *before*
writing it. If it's category 3, 4, or 5, don't write it — flag it for the
owner-decision process in `docs/mark-15-external-standards-adoption-framework.md`
instead. This guide does not grant permission for anything; it only makes
the existing boundary easier to recognize.

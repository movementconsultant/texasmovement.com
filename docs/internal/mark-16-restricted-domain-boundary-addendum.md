# Mark 16 (internal) — Restricted-Domain Boundary Addendum

Internal-only. Not rendered, not imported, not linked from any public route.
Strengthens the restricted/high-risk boundary already established in
`docs/internal/mark-15-vertical-standards-evidence-matrix.md` (items 3 and
4) and `docs/internal/mark-15-public-claims-boundary-guide.md`, using the
governance-only sources researched in Mark 16
(`docs/mark-16-current-best-practice-source-register.md`). **This document
adds no new capability, protocol, or public-release path — it only makes
the existing boundary more specific and better evidenced.**

**Guardrail statement** (applies to every section below, without
exception):

> An external source may inform an internal TMI operating baseline. It does
> not establish that TMI is certified, compliant, evidence-based, safe,
> effective, qualified, operational, or authorized, and it does not
> authorize public claims, service activation, health guidance, legal
> guidance, commerce, checkout, community operations, or product launch.

**This document is not medical, clinical, legal, political, or therapeutic
advice, and does not provide any.** It is an internal governance-process
document only.

## Health and therapy

- **Permitted internal governance topic:** documenting *that* a
  claims-substantiation standard exists and is rigorous (per MK16-SRC-11,
  FTC Health Products Compliance Guidance) — never the substance of any
  health claim itself.
- **Prohibited public or operational activity:** any diagnosis, treatment,
  clinical service, supplement recommendation, recovery program, or
  therapeutic representation, in any form, on any TMI or AVM route.
- **Specialist review required:** a licensed clinical professional, for
  any content even adjacent to this domain, plus separate legal review.
  Both are required together — neither substitutes for the other.
- **Evidence threshold:** Tier 1 (proprietary TMI evidence, owner-provided
  and governance-reviewed) is the only tier that could ever support a
  specific health claim, and even then only after the specialist and
  legal reviews above. Tier 3 (industry best-practice baseline) is
  explicitly insufficient on its own — this is the single clearest
  application of Mark 15's Part D, category 5 ("statements that must
  never be inferred from industry best practices alone").
- **Owner decision required:** whether TMI ever opens a Health/therapy
  function at all. Not addressed, recommended, or assumed by this
  document.
- **Why generic best practice is insufficient:** a best-practice baseline
  describes *how a responsible organization would operate if it had a
  health function* — it says nothing about whether TMI has the licensure,
  insurance, staffing, or evidence to actually claim health-adjacent
  authority. Substituting the baseline for the missing operational reality
  would be exactly the failure mode Tier 4 exists to prevent.
- **Not medical/clinical/legal/political/therapeutic advice:** explicit,
  standalone statement — nothing in this section or its source (MK16-SRC-11)
  should be read as medical or clinical guidance of any kind.

## Tensegrity and sprint/performance claims

- **Permitted internal governance topic:** the scope-of-practice
  distinction between general non-clinical strength/conditioning
  *education* and supervised *coaching*, per MK16-SRC-10 (NSCA governance
  framework, consulted for boundary purposes only). Tensegrity itself is
  treated strictly as a conceptual/modeling lens, never a technique or
  treatment claim, per `docs/internal/mark-15-vertical-standards-evidence-matrix.md`
  item 3.
- **Prohibited public or operational activity:** any tensegrity-as-therapy
  claim, any athlete-therapy or injury-treatment claim, any specific sprint
  technique presented as a TMI proprietary method with an implied outcome,
  and any training protocol of any kind. **This addendum does not contain,
  and this pass did not research, produce, or approve, any training
  protocol, sprint technique, or therapeutic method.**
- **Specialist review required:** credentialed strength/conditioning
  professional review for general education content; licensed clinical
  professional AND legal review, both mandatory, for anything touching
  therapy, injury, or treatment — no exception, regardless of how the
  content is framed.
- **Evidence threshold:** general educational content may draw on Tier 3
  best-practice baselines once reviewed; anything in the therapy/treatment
  space requires Tier 1 evidence plus the specialist/legal gates above,
  which do not exist today.
- **Owner decision required:** whether TMI Performance ever offers
  supervised coaching (as opposed to general education), and separately,
  whether TMI ever pursues a therapy/clinical offering at all — two
  distinct, foundational business decisions this document does not make.
- **Why generic best practice is insufficient:** an NSCA-style scope-of-
  practice framework tells you *where the line is*, not that TMI is on the
  safe side of it. Citing the framework internally is not equivalent to
  having a credentialed coach, an emergency-action plan, or insurance —
  all of which are currently absent.
- **Not medical/clinical/legal/political/therapeutic advice:** explicit,
  standalone statement — nothing in this section constitutes coaching,
  medical, or therapeutic guidance.

## Reparations / legal-political research

- **Permitted internal governance topic:** citation methodology, archival
  custody standards, and corrections-process discipline, per MK16-SRC-12
  (SAA Core Values Statement and Code of Ethics, consulted for boundary
  purposes only). **No specific historical, legal, or political subject
  was researched, summarized, or evaluated in this pass.**
- **Prohibited public or operational activity:** any historical or
  political claim presented as settled fact without a cited, reviewed
  source; any legal advice or legal representation; any content framed as
  advocacy without the owner having made that a deliberate, reviewed
  choice.
- **Specialist review required:** legal review for any published claim
  with legal or political implications, plus a named archival/subject-
  matter reviewer for source methodology — both mandatory, per
  `docs/internal/mark-15-vertical-standards-evidence-matrix.md` item 8.
- **Evidence threshold:** every specific factual claim requires its own
  cited source, reviewed under a documented methodology; a general
  archival-ethics framework (Tier 3-adjacent, professional-body sourced)
  can inform *how* sourcing should work, but never substitutes for the
  sourcing itself.
- **Owner decision required:** whether Reparations operates as editorial/
  research work, advocacy work, or both — a foundational scope decision
  this document does not make, beyond what's already reflected in the one
  existing approved blurb.
- **Why generic best practice is insufficient:** archival ethics standards
  govern process integrity, not the truth or legal weight of any specific
  claim — that determination requires case-specific legal and source
  review no framework can substitute for.
- **Not medical/clinical/legal/political/therapeutic advice:** explicit,
  standalone statement — nothing in this section constitutes legal advice,
  a legal opinion, or a political position.

## Athlete injury/recovery and therapeutic language

- **Permitted internal governance topic:** recognizing this as a subset of
  the Health/therapy boundary above, not a separate lighter-review track —
  athlete-specific framing does not reduce the specialist/legal review
  requirement.
- **Prohibited public or operational activity:** identical to the Health
  and therapy section above — no diagnosis, treatment, recovery program,
  or injury-management claim, athlete-specific or otherwise.
- **Specialist review required:** identical to Health and therapy —
  licensed clinical professional AND legal review, both mandatory.
- **Evidence threshold:** identical to Health and therapy — Tier 1 only,
  after specialist/legal review, never Tier 3 alone.
- **Owner decision required:** identical to Health and therapy — whether
  to open the domain at all.
- **Why generic best practice is insufficient:** identical reasoning —
  a best-practice baseline is not a substitute for licensure, insurance,
  or actual clinical evidence.
- **Not medical/clinical/legal/political/therapeutic advice:** explicit,
  standalone statement.
